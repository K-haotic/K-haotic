My working method comes from construction sites, machine maintenance, gaming, independent study and computational research. A problem does not become simpler because I choose an impressive name for it. It becomes more tractable when I expose the assumptions, identify what is actually observed, and test the boundaries.

## Seven moves

1. **Observe:** What is occurring? What can be directly measured, reproduced, or documented?
2. **Question:** What is unknown? What would have to be true for the current explanation to hold?
3. **Research:** What does existing work already establish? Who knows this system better than I do?
4. **Derive:** Form explanations and models; separate logic and assumptions from intuition and analogy.
5. **Build:** Make the smallest physical, mathematical or software realization that can test the question.
6. **Test:** Compare alternatives, attempt to break the hypothesis, record failures and monitor confounders.
7. **Revise:** Preserve the result, update the model, and design the next experiment.

## Specialization, broad knowledge and curiosity are different

A long career in industrial equipment is practical expertise. Long-standing independent study in biology and robotics is intellectual preparation. An unfamiliar advanced subfield may still require training, supervision or collaboration. I consider those differences useful, not embarrassing: they tell me the right next question.

That is also how I manage AI-assisted development. Models can propose code, analyze data or question an assumption; they are not a replacement for checking sources, reproducing results or accepting responsibility for a physical action. Good collaboration means learning from people and tools while preserving provenance and critical judgment.

## The evidence ladder

| Level | What it means | Example of a boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Question** | A topic or puzzle worth investigating | No result has been demonstrated. |
| **Hypothesis** | A proposed explanation with testable consequences | Plausibility is not confirmation. |
| **Specification** | A design or formal contract | The system may not have been implemented. |
| **Implementation** | A working source artifact or assembled system | Code existing is not proof it ran successfully. |
| **Experiment** | An observed result under recorded conditions | A finite result need not generalize. |
| **Replication / independent evaluation** | A result tested again under declared criteria | Trust grows only within the actual scope of evidence. |
| **Release / operation** | A version admitted to real use | Requires current status and recovery evidence, not an old screenshot. |

The same approach bridges construction and research. A blueprint is not a finished house. A control command is not proof an actuator moved. A function signature is not proof the program is correct. A conceptual model is not a scientific law.
