# THE OLD-SCHOOL GAMER / WHY KHAOTIC EXISTS

[Back to the front door](../README.md) | [Engineering career](ENGINEERING.md) | [Systems map](SYSTEMS.md) | [Khaotic network](NETWORK.md)

I have been a gamer for as long as I can remember. I grew up alongside interactive technology, learning to recognize the patterns behind mechanics, challenge, feedback, competition, cooperation and worlds people could inhabit together.

Gaming is not a side hobby that I attached to an engineering resume. It is one of the foundations of how I think about systems. When I play, I notice the rules, the exceptions, the strategies players discover, the ways worlds remember them, and how design choices change the experience.

My other life was equally hands-on: wood, dirt, steel, machinery, wiring, equipment, maintenance, crews and deadlines. I learned how to build, repair and troubleshoot real systems before applying those instincts to computational ones.

## Where the two paths meet

| From games | Into my development interests |
| --- | --- |
| **Mechanics and rules** | Feedback loops, state, constraints, measurable behavior and simulation. |
| **Worlds and environments** | Immersive environments, geospatial interfaces, XR and community-centered experience design. |
| **Progression and memory** | Persistent systems that retain history, identity and consequences instead of resetting blindly. |
| **Players and social dynamics** | Collaboration, competition, economies, agency, civic systems and meaningful participation. |
| **Modding, testing and exploration mindset** | Experiments that question assumptions and look for what fails at the edges. |

## Khaotic Gaming LLC

I founded Khaotic Gaming LLC in 2022 because I wanted to create, not simply consume. My interests grew from gaming into geospatial/immersive designs, modular computing, AI-assisted environments, experimental hardware and the wider Khaotic network.

Projects such as **OMMI, Whale Fall, Machine City** and related worlds connect this creative ancestry to technical research. Some parts are public and interactive; other parts are prototypes, archival source or plans. **This history is not a claim that every idea has shipped.**

## The builder's promise

If an experience is supposed to remember its participants, coordinate people, learn from events or be trustworthy, its underlying engineering must earn that behavior. That is why I care about firmware, source continuity, logged experiments, governance and recovery as much as about the visible world.

A game can inspire an ambitious system. A laboratory can help test how it should actually work. A builder has to join the two.
